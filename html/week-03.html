The Waterfall Myth: What Royce Actually Wrote

The most-cited diagram in software engineering history is a strawman its own author labeled as risky. Students read Royce 1970 in the original and confront how a field built a fifty-year methodology war on a misreading.

20 minutes instruction  •  25 minutes workshop

By the end of today you can…

  1. Describe the actual argument of Royce's 1970 paper, including the five modifications he proposed to the simple sequential model.
  2. Explain how the misreading happened and what it cost the industry.
  3. Practice reading a primary source before accepting the summary of it - including summaries produced by AI.

Two ways this goes

The same phase of the lifecycle, done well and done badly — both from real projects

When the process holds

Royce's own recommendation

Royce's paper argues for prototyping, early risk retirement, and customer involvement in 1970 - a full 31 years before the Agile Manifesto. Teams that read him rather than the diagram got a decade head start.

When it doesn’t

DoD-STD-2167

The 1985 US Department of Defense standard mandated a rigid document-driven sequential process across defense contracting. It was revised in 1988 and superseded largely because it made iterative development contractually difficult.

AI in the loop

You will use AI this week. Here is where it earns its place — and where it lies to you.

Use it for

Use AI as the subject of the fact-check, not the authority. This is the week where you learn what its failure mode feels like.

Try this prompt

"Summarize Winston Royce's 1970 paper 'Managing the Development of Large Software Systems' in 200 words, and state Royce's own opinion of the sequential model." Then check it against the PDF, line by line.

Do not trust

Many models reproduce the popular myth - that Royce advocated the sequential model - because the myth appears far more often in training data than the paper does. Popularity is not accuracy. This failure mode never fully goes away.

Homework — The Misreading Audit

Estimated time: 75-90 minutes

  1. Read Royce 1970 in full. It is nine pages.
  2. Write a 300-word correction of the most-wrong secondary source you can find online, citing Royce's page numbers.
  3. Find one OTHER widely-repeated software claim you suspect is a misreading. Do not resolve it yet - just log the suspicion and one reason.
  4. AI task: ask an assistant for the Royce summary, then paste in the actual PDF text and ask it to grade its own earlier answer. Record what changed.
